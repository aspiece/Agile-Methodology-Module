## Main ideas

The Agile Manifesto was written in 2001 by people seeking a different way to develop software. It describes Agile as a way of thinking, not a single process that every team must follow.

The Agile mindset is organized through four values and twelve principles. Teams put the mindset into action through practices that fit their work. A practice is useful when it supports the values and principles; simply copying a practice does not make a team Agile.

Agile teams build a culture of learning. They try ideas, gather evidence, and adjust their work. The goal is not only to finish tasks faster. The goal is to understand customer needs and create useful results, even when those needs change.

This approach can support work beyond software development. Teams in many fields can co-create, experiment, adapt, and treat unsuccessful attempts as chances to learn.

## Connection to this lesson

As you read the four Agile values, look for the choices they help a team make. Plans, tools, documents, and contracts still matter. Agile gives more value to people, working results, customer collaboration, and responding to change.
