## Text alternative

The presenter introduces Agile as an approach that helps teams work in flexible ways. Instead of planning every detail at the start and following that plan without change, an Agile team divides work into smaller pieces. The team can deliver useful parts sooner, learn from results, and adjust future work when needs or priorities change.

Agile began in software development, but teams in other fields now use similar ideas. The video explains four main priorities: people and communication, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change. These priorities help a team decide how to work. They do not mean that processes, documentation, agreements, or plans have no value.

The presenter gives Scrum and Kanban as examples of ways teams can organize Agile work. Scrum uses short work periods called Sprints. A Kanban board makes work visible as it moves through stages such as To Do, In Progress, and Done. These practices can help a team track progress, communicate, and deliver value.

The video closes by describing Agile as both a mindset and a set of practices. Agile teams stay open to change, work with customers, communicate often, and keep improving.
