Plan the Next Student Hub Sprint

The Student Hub backlog is ordered, but the team cannot build everything at once.

In this lesson, you help the team move from priority to a realistic Sprint plan.

A strong Sprint plan explains why the selected backlog items fit team capacity, support one Sprint Goal, and still meet the agreed quality standard.

Your Sprint Planning decision should connect the Sprint Goal, team capacity, selected Product Backlog items, and a realistic plan for producing a useful Increment.

This explanation matters because a Sprint plan is stronger when it protects quality and focus instead of filling the Sprint with unrelated tasks.

When you create the Sprint plan, evaluate capacity, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and the expected Increment so the team can explain why the selected work is achievable.

This planning discipline helps Developers maintain focus while still adapting responsibly when new evidence changes the work.

When you defend a Sprint Planning choice, connect stakeholder value, technical feasibility, team capacity, dependencies, and quality expectations so the Developers can inspect whether the plan is realistic before work begins.

📖 Your next move: Read the short explanation below. Look for how planning, feedback, and change work together.

Sprint Planning starts the Sprint by helping the Scrum Team decide why the Sprint is valuable.

That purpose becomes the Sprint Goal. A good Sprint Goal gives the team one shared outcome instead of a loose list of tasks.

The team also looks at the ordered Product Backlog, available capacity, past results, and quality expectations before it selects work.

Capacity means the amount of work the team can realistically handle during the Sprint. The team should not fill every available point. A small buffer helps it respond to normal uncertainty.

The Sprint Backlog contains the Sprint Goal, the selected Product Backlog items, and the Developers' plan for completing the work.

Developers update this plan as they learn. They may change tasks or details without losing focus on the Sprint Goal.

The Sprint Backlog makes the selected work visible. It helps the team see what is planned, what is active, and what still needs attention.

Support Sprint Goal, capacity, and work selection decisions.

During Sprint Planning, the Scrum Team answers three connected questions: why is this Sprint valuable, what can be done, and how will the selected work get started?

For Student Hub, the team may choose a Sprint Goal about improving mobile access. Developers then select work that supports that goal and fits capacity.

Planning must include realistic room for testing, access checks, and unexpected work. These are not extras to add only if time remains.

Step 5.5 · Practical application

Plan the Student Hub Sprint

Estimated time: 14 minutes

Create a Sprint Goal and select work that fits team capacity.

  1. Choose the scenario that best matches your career program interest.
  2. Create one Sprint Goal.
  3. Select work that supports the goal and fits capacity. Keep at least 2 capacity points for testing and unexpected work.
  4. List one dependency or risk.
  5. Explain why your plan is achievable and how it protects quality.
