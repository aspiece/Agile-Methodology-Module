Lesson overview

Agile Foundations: Responding to Change

In this lesson, you will learn what Agile is and why teams use it. You will examine the four Agile Manifesto values. You will compare Agile and predictive ways of responding to change. You will also explain how short cycles, feedback, and small product increments can lower risk.

Learning objectives

  • L1 O1 Explain how Agile values guide decisions when needs change.
  • L1 O2 Compare Agile and predictive responses to changing user needs.
  • L1 O3 Explain how short cycles, feedback, and small product increments reduce risk.

Student Hub is a work in progress app for assignments, deadlines, and course events. You are joining the product team.

Across eight lessons, you will help guide each update. You will listen to users, organize work, test ideas, and improve the app as new evidence appears.

As you compare choices, use evidence, product value, and risk to justify why a small tested increment should come before less urgent work.

Learn

📖 Your next move: Read the short explanation below. Look for how planning, feedback, and change work together.

LEARN, Software teams do not know every detail at the start. Agile helps them learn while they build. In each short cycle, a team plans, builds, tests, and gets feedback. Then the team uses that evidence to improve the product.

One cycle is called an iteration. A usable piece of the product is an increment. Feedback can come from users, stakeholders, tests, or teammates. Agile teams still plan, record key choices, and protect quality. They change the plan when evidence points to a better path.

Now connect the cycle to Student Hub. Students report that the calendar does not work well on a phone. The team can study that feedback and plan a small fix. This choice protects quality and responds to a real user need.

The Agile Mindset: A Different Way of Working

Introduce the Agile Manifesto as a mindset shaped by four values, twelve principles, learning, experimentation, and customer focus.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

The Agile Manifesto helps teams make choices during software work. It is a mindset, not a single required process. Plans, tools, documents, and contracts still matter. However, Agile teams give more value to the ideas on the left:

  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
  • Working software over comprehensive documentation.
  • Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
  • Responding to change over following a plan.

For Student Hub, the team should use feedback when the plan no longer fits. The team can keep its plan and notes. User evidence should guide the next useful improvement.

Watch the team make an Agile decision

The team planned to add profile themes. A quick test shows that assignment due dates are hard to scan on small screens. Follow the team’s decision from evidence to a small test.

New evidence
Students miss due dates because key information is hard to scan.
Predictive response
Keep building profile themes because they are next in the original plan.
Agile response
Move the due date display ahead because the evidence shows a more urgent user need.
Agile value
Responding to change over following a plan.
Small increment
Improve one assignment card so its due date is clear on a small screen.
How testing lowers risk
Test the revised card with a few users before changing the whole app.

Your selected program: Software Development

Situation: The Student Hub team planned to build event reminders next. Testing now shows the calendar is hard to use on a phone, and keyboard users cannot reach the date filter.

A strong response says a predictive team might keep building reminders because they are in the plan. An Agile team would use the access evidence to move a calendar fix ahead. It might test a keyboard ready date filter first. This small test lowers the risk of building more features on a calendar that some students cannot use.

Your selected program: Cybersecurity

Situation: A security team planned to add a new report. A test shows that users stay signed in on shared lab computers after they close the browser.

A strong response says a predictive team might finish the report first. An Agile team would move the sign out risk ahead because it could expose user accounts. It might test automatic sign out on one shared device workflow. The test lowers risk before the change is used across the system.

Your selected program: Data Science

Situation: A data team planned to add a new chart. A review shows that the dashboard uses old data but does not show when the data was last updated.

A strong response says a predictive team might build the chart first. An Agile team would use the review evidence to make data age clear. It might add and test a last updated label on one dashboard. The test lowers the risk that users make choices with old data.

Your selected program: Artificial Intelligence

Situation: An AI team planned to add longer study summaries. User tests show that some current summaries leave out key facts.

A strong response says a predictive team might build longer summaries first. An Agile team would use the test evidence to improve accuracy. It might test the current summary on a small set of checked examples. The test lowers the risk of giving learners incomplete information.

Your selected program: Game Development

Situation: A game team planned to build a new level. Playtests show that some players cannot remap the controls they need to use.

A strong response says a predictive team might finish the new level first. An Agile team would act on the control feedback. It might let players remap one set of core actions and test it. The test lowers the risk of building more content that some players cannot use.

Your selected program: Physical Computing

Situation: A device team planned to add a new display. Tests show that its sensor sometimes reports a safe temperature when the device is too warm.

A strong response says a predictive team might build the display first. An Agile team would address the sensor evidence before adding features. It might test sensor calibration and a safe shutoff on one device. The test lowers the risk of harm from a false reading.
