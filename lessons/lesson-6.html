Daily Scrum, Teamwork, and Blockers

The Daily Scrum is a 15 minute event for the Developers. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog.

The Daily Scrum is not a status report to a manager. Developers use it to plan the next workday. They choose the format that helps them inspect progress and make a useful adjustment.

A strong Daily Scrum update connects work to the Sprint Goal, names evidence, and makes a blocker clear. A blocker is anything that slows or stops needed work.

Step 6.3 · Learn and check

Daily Stand Ups in the Workplace

Estimated time: 6 minutes

▶️ Your next move: Watch for how short team check ins make work visible. Notice the connection between a stand up, a Daily Scrum, and the team's plan for the next workday.

Agile, Explained: Daily Standup Meetings

Focus Daily Scrum communication on progress, planning, and blockers.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

Many workplaces use the phrase daily stand up for a short team check in. In Scrum, the event is called the Daily Scrum. The Scrum name matters because it has a clear purpose: Developers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan.

This distinction helps the team avoid a common mistake. The event is not a full status meeting, a long problem solving session, or a report to a boss. It is most useful when team members share the information needed to keep important, time sensitive work moving.

When a team practices self management, Developers organize their own work instead of waiting for one person to assign every task. A clear stand up update gives the team enough information to choose a responsible next action.

Step 6.4 · Learn and check

Progress, Blockers, and Adaptation

Estimated time: 12 minutes

▶️ Your next move: Watch for the difference between a blocker the team can handle and an impediment that needs help from someone outside the team.

YDS: When Does a Scrum Master Remove an Impediment?

Clarify when a blocker can be handled by Developers and when an impediment needs support from outside the team.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

A blocker is a problem that stops or slows needed work. Some blockers can be handled by the Developers. For example, Developers might pair on difficult work, ask for needed information, or reorder tasks in the Sprint Backlog.

An impediment is a barrier that the Scrum Team cannot remove on its own. The Scrum Master helps when the problem is outside the team's authority or ability. The Scrum Master supports the team, but does not assign the Developers' work.

Transparency means making important work and problems visible. Inspection means reviewing the current evidence. Adaptation means changing the plan when the evidence shows a need. During or after the Daily Scrum, Developers may update the Sprint Backlog so the plan still protects the Sprint Goal.

Some problems need a longer discussion after the Daily Scrum. The team should keep the event focused, record a clear next action, and involve the people who can help. This keeps the Daily Scrum useful while giving larger problems the time and attention they deserve.

The important decision is not who sounds busiest. The important decision is whether the current evidence changes the team's plan. When Developers name a blocker, explain its impact, and choose a specific follow up, they protect the Sprint Goal without turning the Daily Scrum into a lecture or a manager led status meeting.

Turn a vague update into useful evidence

A vague update makes it hard for the team to adapt. A useful update gives enough evidence for the team to choose a next action.

Vague update
"I am still working on keyboard testing."
Useful update
"The keyboard test is blocked because the test account is missing. This affects the Sprint Goal because we cannot confirm the date filter is accessible. I need the test account today, or another Developer can pair with me on mobile layout testing."
Why it works
The update names the blocker, connects it to the Sprint Goal, and suggests a next action.

Step 6.5 · Practical application

Respond to Student Hub Blockers

Estimated time: 12 minutes

Use Daily Scrum evidence to adapt a team plan.

  1. Choose the scenario that best matches your career program interest.
  2. Identify the blocker and the work that threatens the Sprint Goal.
  3. Write a concise Daily Scrum update that names evidence and asks for the help the team needs.
  4. Choose three next actions for the Developers. Include one Sprint Backlog change and one person who can help remove the blocker.
  5. Explain how your actions protect the Sprint Goal.

Your selected program: Software Development

Situation: The Student Hub team has a blocked keyboard test because the test account is missing. The mobile layout is complete but untested, announcement search is on track, and a Developer started an unrelated theme change.

A strong response makes the missing account visible, returns focus to the Sprint Goal, schedules testing, updates the plan, and seeks help without making the Scrum Master the team's boss.

Your selected program: Cybersecurity

Situation: The team is testing automatic sign out on shared computers. The test account is locked, so the security test cannot continue. Another Developer starts changing button colors even though the Sprint Goal is account safety.

A strong response makes the locked account visible, keeps account safety as the focus, asks for help from the person who manages test accounts, and moves unrelated design work behind the security work.

Your selected program: Data Science

Situation: The team is fixing a stale Student Hub dashboard. The data refresh script fails, so the latest assignment data does not load. A Developer starts building a new chart before the data quality issue is solved.

A strong response names the failed refresh as the blocker, protects the goal of trustworthy data, asks for help with the script or data source, and pauses extra chart work until the data is reliable.

Your selected program: Artificial Intelligence

Situation: The team is testing an AI study helper summary. The review set is missing correct answers, so the team cannot check whether the summary leaves out key facts. A Developer starts drafting a new chatbot greeting.

A strong response makes the missing answer set visible, keeps accuracy as the Sprint Goal risk, asks for the review data, and moves greeting work behind the quality check.

Your selected program: Game Development

Situation: The team is testing remappable controls for a Student Hub practice game. The controller test device is unavailable, so the team cannot confirm the controls work. A Developer starts designing a new badge animation.

A strong response names the missing test device, keeps access and playability as the goal, asks for the device or an approved substitute, and delays badge animation until controls are tested.

Your selected program: Physical Computing

Situation: The team is testing a sensor alert that sends status to Student Hub. The lab device will not connect, so the team cannot confirm the alert is accurate. A Developer starts changing the dashboard theme.

A strong response makes the device connection blocker visible, protects the goal of accurate alerts, asks for technical help or a backup device, and keeps theme work behind safety related testing.
