Lesson overview

Review, Feedback, and Continuous Improvement

In this lesson, you will use Sprint Review feedback to improve the product and retrospective evidence to improve the team's process. You will keep product feedback and process feedback separate so each one leads to the right next action.

Learning objectives

  • L7 O1 Use Sprint Review feedback to recommend a Product Backlog change.
  • L7 O2 Use retrospective evidence to choose one team process improvement to test next Sprint.
  • L7 O3 Explain the difference between product feedback and process feedback.

LEARN, The Sprint Review is a Scrum event for inspecting the product result. The Scrum Team and stakeholders look at what was built, what changed, and what should happen next.

The Sprint Review is not only a presentation. It is a working session. Stakeholder feedback may lead the Product Owner to reorder the Product Backlog.

For Student Hub, students may report that search works but the results are hard to scan on a phone. The team should not promise every request. It should compare the feedback with product value, risk, and the Product Goal.

Product feedback answers this question: What did we learn about the product or the people who use it?

Sprint Retrospective Improvements

Sprint Retrospective, Scrum Foundations eLearning Series

Explain Sprint Retrospective as process inspection and improvement.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

The Sprint Retrospective helps the Scrum Team improve how it works. Team members inspect people, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done.

The team discusses what went well, what caused problems, and what change may help. The retrospective is not used to blame a person. It focuses on learning and shared improvement.

A useful improvement is specific and small enough to test during the next Sprint. For example, the team might review acceptance criteria together before starting each backlog item.

Process feedback answers this question: What did we learn about how the team worked?

Step 7.4 · Practical application

Use Feedback to Improve Student Hub

Estimated time: 15 minutes

Separate product feedback from process feedback. Then choose one product action and one team process action.

  1. Choose the scenario that best matches your career program interest. Sort each finding into product feedback or process feedback. Recommend one Product Backlog change from the product evidence. Write one specific process improvement to test during the next Sprint. Explain how each action uses evidence. End with this handoff: Our team will improve the product by ___ and improve our process by ___.

Review and retrospective response template

Use this structure to keep product feedback and process feedback separate.

  • Product feedback, What did stakeholders or users show about the product?
  • Product Backlog change, What should the Product Owner consider ordering next?
  • Process feedback, What did the team learn about how it worked?
  • Process improvement, What small team habit or practice should be tested next Sprint?
  • Evidence, How does each action connect to the evidence?

Complete one career scenario. You may choose another afterward for optional practice.

Your selected program: Software Development

Situation: Student Hub testers say mobile search results are hard to scan. Teachers ask for filters by course. The team also finds that unclear acceptance criteria caused repeated testing.

A strong response puts mobile search and course filters in the Sprint Review lane because they are product feedback. It puts unclear acceptance criteria in the retrospective lane because it is about how the team worked. A useful process improvement could be to review acceptance criteria together before starting each backlog item.

Your selected program: Cybersecurity

Situation: Student testers say the password reset screen does not explain what makes a safe password. A support stakeholder asks for clearer account lock messages. The team also notices that security checks were added late in the Sprint.

A strong response treats password guidance and account lock messages as product feedback. It treats late security checks as process evidence. A useful process improvement could be to review security acceptance criteria before development starts.

Your selected program: Data Science

Situation: Dashboard users say assignment trends are useful but hard to compare by course. A stakeholder asks for a last updated label. The team also finds that data quality questions were not discussed until testing.

A strong response treats course comparison and the last updated label as product feedback. It treats late data quality discussion as process evidence. A useful process improvement could be to add a data quality check to each backlog item before work begins.

Your selected program: Artificial Intelligence

Situation: Student testers say an AI study summary feature is helpful, but some summaries leave out required details. A teacher asks for a confidence note. The team also finds that test examples were chosen too late.

A strong response treats summary accuracy and the confidence note as product feedback. It treats late test example selection as process evidence. A useful process improvement could be to choose review examples during Sprint Planning.

Your selected program: Game Development

Situation: Players say the tutorial level is fun but the controls are hard to learn. A stakeholder asks for clearer progress feedback. The team also finds that playtest notes were not reviewed until the end.

A strong response treats controls and progress feedback as product feedback. It treats late playtest review as process evidence. A useful process improvement could be to review playtest notes during a mid Sprint team check.

Your selected program: Physical Computing

Situation: Device testers say the reminder light is easy to see, but the alert sound is too quiet in a busy room. A stakeholder asks for a battery warning. The team also finds that hardware tests were not scheduled early enough.

A strong response treats alert volume and battery warning as product feedback. It treats late hardware testing as process evidence. A useful process improvement could be to schedule a hardware test before the final review.
