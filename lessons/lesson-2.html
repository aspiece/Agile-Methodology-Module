LEARN, Scrum is one Agile framework. A framework is a simple structure a team can use to organize work. Scrum does not replace Agile values. It gives the team clear roles, events, and artifacts. These parts help the team see the work.

A Scrum Team works toward one Product Goal. The team plans a short cycle of work. It creates a usable product Increment. Then the team inspects feedback and adapts what comes next. Scrum helps Student Hub stay focused as needs change.

Scrum Team Accountabilities

Estimated time: 10 minutes

▶️ Your next move: Watch for how each Scrum role helps the Student Hub team make a different decision.

Scrum Roles

Introduce Scrum roles/accountabilities with a short focused video.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

A Scrum Team has three accountabilities. An accountability is an area of responsibility. It is not a job rank. The three accountabilities are Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers.

The Product Owner is accountable for product value and Product Backlog order. This person decides which outcomes and backlog items matter most.

Developers create each usable Increment. They decide how to organize, build, and test the selected work.

The Scrum Master helps the team understand and use Scrum well. This person coaches the team and helps the team address barriers.

Stakeholders are not members of the Scrum Team. They share needs, evidence, and feedback. For Student Hub, stakeholders may include students, teachers, families, office staff, or support staff.

Watch the team choose the right role

The Student Hub team has four decisions to make. Each decision points to a different Scrum role or to stakeholders.

Product decision
Mobile access may need to move above a theme selector. The Product Owner leads backlog order because this choice affects product value.
Work decision
The calendar update is selected for the Sprint. Developers decide how to build and test the work.
Barrier
A missing test account blocks progress. The Scrum Master helps make the barrier visible. The team works with the right people to remove it.
User evidence
Student testers describe confusing navigation. Stakeholders give feedback that helps the team understand the product.
Team rule
The Scrum Master supports the process. The Scrum Master does not become the team's boss.

Check for understanding

Student testers say the calendar is hard to use on a phone. Which group gives this feedback?

Step 2.4 · Learn and check

Cross Functional and Self Organizing Teams

Estimated time: 7 minutes

▶️ Your next move: Watch for why a Scrum Team needs different skills and why Developers help organize their own work.

Cross Functional and Self Organizing Teams, Scrum Foundations eLearning Series

Reinforce cross functional, self managing teamwork.

Open video in new tabOpen transcript

A cross functional team has the skills needed to create a usable product Increment. The team may need coding, design, testing, data, security, accessibility, and communication skills. Not every person needs every skill. The team needs enough skills across the group.

A self managing team decides how to organize its work. Developers do not wait for a boss to assign every task. They plan together, make work visible, ask for help, and adjust the plan when they learn more.

For Student Hub, the team may need one person to improve the calendar layout, another to test keyboard access, and another to check account data. The team works together around one goal instead of handing work from one silo to another.

Check for understanding
